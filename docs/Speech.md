# JustDrinks LTD analysis: speech

About 8 minutes of talking at 140 words a minute, plus pauses to point at charts and a short question break. That fills a 10-minute slot. Each section names its slide. Every figure was checked against the Power BI model.

## Slide 1: Title (about 15 seconds)

Thank you for having me. I'm Brandy Sango. I'm going to walk you through my analysis of JustDrinks Ltd.: a year of sales, purchasing and inventory for an 80-store drinks retailer, and what the leadership team should do next.

## Slide 2: The brief (about 45 seconds)

JustDrinks is a retailer of wine and spirits with about 80 stores. Over the last twelve months it sold $452 million.

The leadership team asked three things. Where does the money come from? How well is the company buying and holding stock? And which vendors, stores and products drive profit?

I answered them in a Power BI report of 12 pages, which follows the questions in order.

## Slide 3: The data (about 40 seconds)

The data comes from four sources for the 2016 financial year: sales, purchases, and the stock counts at the start and end of the year.

There are about 12.8 million sales lines and 2.4 million purchase lines, across 80 stores and 68 cities.

Two things to keep in mind. Stock is counted only twice, at the start and the end of the year, so I can't track turnover month by month. And gross profit here means sales minus purchases, so the timing of buying affects it.

## Slide 4: Executive summary (about 50 seconds)

This is the Executive Summary page of the report. Sales were $452 million and gross profit was $130 million, a margin of 28.8 percent. Purchases were $322 million.

The line chart shows sales in black and purchases in orange. Sales rise through the year and peak in July and December. Purchases peak in July and October, then fall.

That gap at the end of the year is the main finding, and I'll come back to it.

## Slide 5: Spirits and wine (about 45 seconds)

Spirits make 64.19 percent of sales, $290 million. Wine makes 35.81 percent, $162 million.

One technical note. The category codes were labelled the wrong way round in the original model. I checked the bottle sizes to confirm it: the group labelled wine had 50mL minis and 1750mL bottles, which are spirits sizes, and the other group had 5-litre boxes and 187mL four-packs, which are wine. I corrected it, so the report and these numbers now agree.

## Slide 6: Bottle sizes (about 45 seconds)

Two sizes carry the business. The 750mL bottle brings in $254 million and the 1750mL bottle $132 million. Together that is 85 percent of sales.

Wine sells most in 750mL, $131 million and 10.2 million units. Spirits sell most in 1750mL by dollars, $132 million, and by units they are level across 50mL, 750mL and 1750mL, at about 6 million each.

## Slide 7: Stores and cities (about 50 seconds)

Stores differ a lot. Sales per store run from $0.4 million to $25.5 million. Doncaster is the top city at $47.2 million.

Prices differ too. For spirits, the lowest average price is at store 29, $10.51, and the highest at store 76, $18.75. For wine, store 45 is lowest at $10.23 and store 3 highest at $18.68.

The best stores are worth studying and copying.

## Slide 8: Vendors (about 45 seconds)

Buying is concentrated. Diageo is the largest vendor by value, $51.0 million, and by quantity, 5.5 million units. That is 15.8 percent of all purchases.

Martignetti is next at $27.9 million, then Jim Beam, Pernod Ricard and Bacardi. The top five vendors together are 45 percent of purchases.

That is useful buying power for negotiating terms, and a risk if one supplier fails.

## Slide 9: Sales against purchases (about 60 seconds)

This is the finding I'd act on first. From October, sales climb and purchases fall.

In December, sales were $52.3 million and purchases $26.3 million, half as much. In units, the company sold 3.8 million bottles and bought 2.8 million. It drew down about a million units of stock in the busiest month.

December also has the highest margin, 49.6 percent, against 12.6 percent in October. Part of that is real demand, and part is buying timing, because gross profit is sales minus purchases.

## Slide 10: Recommendations (about 65 seconds)

Five things to do.

One: buy holiday stock earlier, so it is in place before November and December.

Two: protect the 750mL and 1750mL sizes. They are 85 percent of sales.

Three: use vendor volume to negotiate, and line up a back-up supplier.

Four: learn from the best stores and cities, and share their practices with the weakest.

Five: track profit by product, and review the slowest brands. Some take close to a year to make a first sale.

## Slide 11: Conclusion (about 40 seconds)

JustDrinks has a strong revenue base. The clearest gains are in timing, mix and store results, not in finding new sales.

Two limits to say out loud. Stock is counted only twice a year, so I would ask for monthly counts to track turnover properly. And the data shows what happened, not why, so the first step is to ask store managers why the best stores sell and price the way they do.

Thank you. I'm happy to take questions.

## Likely questions

**What is the total sales, and the wine and spirits split?** $452.1 million. Spirits are $290.2 million (64.19%) and wine $161.9 million (35.81%).

**What is the most popular size for wine and for spirits?** Wine: 750mL, by dollars ($131.1 million) and by units (10.2 million). Spirits: 1750mL by dollars ($132.1 million), and level with 50mL and 750mL by units.

**Which stores have the highest and lowest average price?** Spirits: highest store 76 ($18.75), lowest store 29 ($10.51). Wine: highest store 3 ($18.68), lowest store 45 ($10.23).

**Which vendors contribute most to purchases?** Diageo, by value and by quantity. Martignetti, Jim Beam, Pernod Ricard and Bacardi follow by value.

**Which vendors are critical suppliers?** The report ranks vendors by purchase value, with order count as the tie-breaker. Diageo ranks first, then Martignetti, Jim Beam, Pernod Ricard, Bacardi and Constellation.

**How does inventory purchased compare with inventory sold?** Purchases ran ahead of sales in most months, but in November and December sales overtook them, with a drawdown of about 1.2 million units.

**Which brands have the longest shelf time?** Some wine brands take 280 to 340 days to make a first sale. Category averages are 9.1 days for wine and 6.2 days for spirits.

**How does inventory turnover relate to sales and margin?** Turnover is 7.2 times a year, about 50 days of stock, but stock is counted only twice a year, so it can't be tracked by month. Sales and margin both peak in December.

**Why did you correct the wine and spirits labels?** The sizes made it clear. Spirits sizes (50mL, 1750mL) were labelled wine, and wine sizes (5-litre boxes, 187mL four-packs) were labelled spirits.

**What would you do first?** Buy holiday stock earlier. It is the biggest gap in the data, and it needs no new customers.
